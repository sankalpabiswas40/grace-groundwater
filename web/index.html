1 · The idea

Gravity is not the same everywhere

Gravity depends on how much mass is beneath you. Water is heavy: when an aquifer fills, gravity above it becomes very slightly stronger. When it is pumped out, gravity weakens. The change is tiny, but it can be measured.

Drag the slider to fill or empty the aquifer and watch the gravimeter.

Earth's gravity is about 980,000,000 µGal.

since 1 µGal = 10−8 m s−2. So each centimetre of water changes gravity by about 0.42 µGal. This number is checked in the project's tests.

2 · The instrument

How two satellites feel a mass

Two satellites follow each other around the Earth. When the leader approaches extra mass, it is pulled forward first and the gap between them grows. Then the follower is pulled too and catches up. The satellites measure that tiny change in distance.

Keep scrolling to fly the pair over the mass.

0.0 µm change in separation

Go deeper: how the distance is measured

Microwave ranging (GRACE and GRACE-FO). NASA states the original GRACE satellites "were able to measure their separation distance to within one micron" while flying about 220 km apart at an altitude of roughly 500 km. NASA/JPL: GRACE-FO spacecraft.

Laser ranging interferometer (GRACE-FO only). A technology demonstration that NASA says "could improve the accuracy of inter-spacecraft ranging by tenfold or more" (same NASA page).

GPS and accelerometers. GPS tracks each orbit, and accelerometers measure non-gravity forces such as air drag and sunlight pressure so they can be removed, leaving only the effect of gravity.

3 · The recipe

From gravity to groundwater

GRACE does not see groundwater directly. It weighs all the water in a region. We subtract the parts we can model to find what is left underground.

  1. Satellite rangingdistance between the twins, every few seconds
  2. Monthly gravity fieldone global solution per month
  3. Masconsmass blocks about 300 km wide
  4. Total water storageeverything: soil, snow, rivers, groundwater
  5. − soil moisture, snow, canopyfrom the GLDAS Noah land model
  6. = Groundwaterwhat remains, with the model's errors

Pick a month: where is the water anomaly, NW India?

Values are anomalies in cm of water relative to the 2004–2009 average, area-weighted over Punjab, Haryana, Delhi and Rajasthan. Total water storage: JPL mascons. Groundwater = total − soil moisture − snow − canopy.

Go deeper: the subtraction

All terms are anomalies relative to January 2004 – December 2009, in cm of water. Soil moisture is the sum of GLDAS-2.1 Noah's four layers (0–200 cm). Surface water in rivers, canals and reservoirs is not removed, so it is still inside our "groundwater" number. Any error in the land model goes straight into the groundwater estimate.

Go deeper: how to read the columns
  • Columns stand on a base of 60 cm, so a cell at the 2004–2009 average is 60 cm × the exaggeration tall, and a cell 60 cm below average is flat. Height = (60 cm + anomaly) × exaggeration.
  • The data are the same monthly JPL mascon frames as the flat map (gain factors applied). Neighbouring columns move together because the real measurement is a ~300 km mascon.
  • Months with no satellite solution show "No data"; nothing is interpolated.
  • The white outline is our analysis region (Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Rajasthan). It floats above the columns so it stays visible; its height means nothing.

Go deeper: what the map can and cannot show
  • Monthly frames are the JPL mascon solution with JPL's gain factors applied, sampled on a 0.5° grid. The native measurement is a 3° mascon, so neighbouring cells are not independent. You can see the mascon blocks in the trend map.
  • Trend maps use the JPL solution without gain factors, because JPL cautions that the gain factors are designed around the seasonal cycle.
  • A few cells in the high Himalaya (near 28°N, 87°E) show large positive "groundwater" trends. This is very likely snow accumulating in the GLDAS model at glacier cells, not real groundwater. We left it visible.

5 · The result

The monsoon breathes, the trend falls

Every year the monsoon refills the ground and the dry season drains it. Underneath that rhythm, the long-term level in north-west India has been dropping.
