1 · The idea

Gravity is not the same everywhere

Gravity depends on how much mass is beneath you. Water is heavy: when an aquifer fills, gravity above it becomes very slightly stronger. When it is pumped out, gravity weakens. The change is tiny, but it can be measured.

Drag the slider to fill or empty the aquifer and watch the gravimeter.

If gravity alone shaped the sea

Because mass inside the Earth is uneven, a calm ocean would not settle into a perfect ellipsoid. It would sit higher where gravity is stronger and lower where it is weaker. That surface is the geoid. South of India it dips by about , the deepest hollow on the planet.

We take the spherical-harmonic coefficients of the EGM2008 gravity model from the ICGEM service at GFZ Potsdam and keep only degrees 2 to 60. After removing the field of the GRS80 reference ellipsoid, the disturbing potential T on the ellipsoid gives the geoid height by the Bruns formula:

The globe draws each point at radius 1 + (exaggeration × N ÷ 6371 km). At 1× the Earth looks perfectly round: about 105 m is nothing against 6371 km.

Limits: the model is cut at degree 60 (features smaller than about 300 km are missing), it is the tide-free version, and small corrections (degree-0 term, topography) are not applied. Model: Pavlis, Holmes, Kenyon & Factor (2012), doi:10.1029/2011JB008916.

What GRACE adds: this shape is the long-term average. GRACE and GRACE-FO measure how it changes from month to month as water moves, and those changes are what the rest of this page is about.

2 · The instrument

How two satellites feel a mass

Two satellites follow each other around the Earth. When the leader approaches extra mass, it is pulled forward first and the gap between them grows. Then the follower is pulled too and catches up. The satellites measure that tiny change in distance.

Keep scrolling to fly the pair over the mass.

0.0 µm change in separation

Go deeper: how the distance is measured

Microwave ranging (GRACE and GRACE-FO). NASA states the original GRACE satellites "were able to measure their separation distance to within one micron" while flying about 220 km apart at an altitude of roughly 500 km. NASA/JPL: GRACE-FO spacecraft.

Laser ranging interferometer (GRACE-FO only). A technology demonstration that NASA says "could improve the accuracy of inter-spacecraft ranging by tenfold or more" (same NASA page).

GPS and accelerometers. GPS tracks each orbit, and accelerometers measure non-gravity forces such as air drag and sunlight pressure so they can be removed, leaving only the effect of gravity.

Inside GRACE-FO

3 · The recipe

From gravity to groundwater

GRACE does not see groundwater directly. It weighs all the water in a region. We subtract the parts we can model to find what is left underground.

  1. Satellite rangingdistance between the twins, every few seconds
  2. Monthly gravity fieldone global solution per month
  3. Masconsmass blocks about 300 km wide
  4. Total water storageeverything: soil, snow, rivers, groundwater
  5. − soil moisture, snow, canopyfrom the GLDAS Noah land model
  6. = Groundwaterwhat remains, with the model's errors

Pick a month: where is the water anomaly, NW India?

Values are anomalies in cm of water relative to the 2004–2009 average, area-weighted over Punjab, Haryana, Delhi and Rajasthan. Total water storage: JPL mascons. Groundwater = total − soil moisture − snow − canopy.

Go deeper: the subtraction

All terms are anomalies relative to January 2004 – December 2009, in cm of water. Soil moisture is the sum of GLDAS-2.1 Noah's four layers (0–200 cm). Surface water in rivers, canals and reservoirs is not removed, so it is still inside our "groundwater" number. Any error in the land model goes straight into the groundwater estimate.

Go deeper: how to read the columns
  • Columns stand on a base of 60 cm, so a cell at the 2004–2009 average is 60 cm × the exaggeration tall, and a cell 60 cm below average is flat. Height = (60 cm + anomaly) × exaggeration.
  • Data: monthly JPL mascons (gain factors applied), processed the same way as the flat map but for the whole of India, stored in whole centimetres. Only grid cells at least half inside India are shown. Neighbouring columns move together because the real measurement is a ~300 km mascon.
  • About 45 cells in the Himalaya and north-east India have very large values (up to about 360 cm) where JPL's gain factors are large. The compact 3D file stores anything beyond ±127 cm as ±127 cm and the popup says so. Colours already saturate at ±40 cm.
  • Between months the columns glide for about a third of a second. That glide is animation only; every resting frame is a real monthly value.
  • Months with no satellite solution show "No data"; nothing is interpolated.
  • Thin lines are the state boundaries of India (DataMeet India community file, CC BY 4.0). They float above the columns so they stay visible; their height means nothing.
  • The thick black outline is our analysis region (Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Rajasthan).

3D · Under the ground

What does that mean for the water table?

GRACE gives a thickness of water. In the ground, water only fills the gaps between grains, so the same loss lowers the water table by much more. How much more depends on the specific yield (Sy): the fraction of the aquifer's volume that drains freely.

This is an estimate, not a measurement. It is a regional average; real wells differ widely.

Go deeper: the conversion and where Sy comes from

ΔGWS is our NW India groundwater storage anomaly for the month (JPL mascons minus GLDAS, cm of water, relative to 2004–2009).

Default Sy = 0.12 is the value assumed for this same region by Rodell et al. (2009), who cite India's Ministry of Water Resources: "Assuming a specific yield of 0.12", they estimate a regional mean water-table decline of about 0.33 m per year. Nature 460, 999–1002.

Slider range 0.03 to 0.32 spans the individual determinations for silt through medium sand in Johnson (1967), Specific yield: compilation of specific yields for various materials, USGS Water-Supply Paper 1662-D, doi:10.3133/wsp1662D:
