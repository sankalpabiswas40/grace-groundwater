1  Gravity changes with water

Gravity is slightly stronger above more mass. Water is heavy, so a full aquifer pulls a little harder than an empty one. For a wide flat layer of water of thickness h (the Bouguer slab):

Δg = 2πGρh  ⇒  µGal per cm of water

Earth's gravity is about 980,000,000 µGal, so the signal is tiny but measurable.

2  Two satellites feel the pull

The pair orbits about 500 km up and tracks its separation to about a micron. Extra mass ahead pulls the leader first, so the gap grows, then shrinks as the follower catches up. A month of such measurements gives one gravity map of the Earth.

Schematic, not to scale. Distances from NASA/JPL.

3  From gravity to groundwater

GRACE weighs all the water in a region (total water storage, TWS). We subtract the parts a land model can estimate:

ΔGWS = ΔTWS − (soil moisture + snow + canopy water)

5  What to keep in mind

  • GRACE sees features about 300 km across; our region is only about a dozen independent measurements.
  • Groundwater is what is left after subtracting a model, so it carries the model's errors. Rivers and reservoirs are not removed.
  • The ± values are formal fit errors and a lower bound; the spread between solutions is the more honest guide.
  • A straight line simplifies the record: the fall is steady to about 2017 and flatter, with large swings, afterwards.
  • The satellites show that water is lost, not who used it. Rodell et al. (2009) attribute the loss mainly to pumping for irrigation.
